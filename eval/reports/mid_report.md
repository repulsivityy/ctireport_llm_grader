# Threat Briefing: Ransomware Activity Against the Logistics Sector — Q3 2026

**Prepared for:** Senior Management
**Author:** Threat Intelligence Team
**Date:** 28 August 2026

## Executive Summary

Ransomware groups have increased their focus on logistics and freight companies
over the last quarter. Several operators, including the group tracked as
BlackHaul, have hit transport and warehousing firms in Europe and North America.
We believe it is likely that this trend will continue and that a company in our
sector could be affected in the coming months. Management should be aware of this
risk and consider whether current defences are sufficient.

The main entry points have been internet-facing remote access systems and
phishing. Once inside, the attackers move quickly to encrypt systems and steal
data for extortion.

## Scope

This briefing covers ransomware threats to the logistics sector during Q3 2026.
It is based on open-source reporting and vendor threat intelligence.

## Key Developments

- BlackHaul has claimed responsibility for at least six attacks on logistics
  firms since June, according to their leak site and reporting from Recorded
  Future.
- A widely reported incident at a European freight forwarder in July caused
  several days of shipping delays. The company disclosed that customer data was
  stolen.
- Industry reporting suggests ransom demands in the sector have ranged from one
  to five million dollars.
- Attackers are increasingly exploiting vulnerabilities in VPN appliances. A
  number of the affected companies were running unpatched versions.
- There has been more use of "double extortion" where data is both encrypted and
  leaked.

## Analysis

The logistics sector is attractive to ransomware operators because downtime is
very costly and companies are under pressure to restore operations quickly,
which may make them more likely to pay. Many firms in the sector also run older
technology and have complex operational systems that are hard to secure.

We assess that our organisation has similar exposure to the companies that have
been attacked. We operate VPN infrastructure for remote access and have a large
workforce that could be targeted by phishing. It is possible that we have gaps
in our patching of perimeter devices.

There is some uncertainty in this assessment because much of the reporting comes
from the attackers themselves and from vendors, and the true number of incidents
may be higher than what is publicly known.

## Business Impact

A successful ransomware attack could disrupt our warehouse management and
transport scheduling systems, leading to delivery delays and financial loss. There
could also be regulatory consequences if customer data is stolen, and reputational
damage with key clients.

## Recommendations

- Review and prioritise patching of all internet-facing remote access systems.
- Assess the coverage and configuration of our EDR tooling across the estate.
- Run a phishing simulation campaign for staff in high-risk roles.
- Review our incident response and backup restoration procedures.
- Consider briefing the board on ransomware risk to the sector.
